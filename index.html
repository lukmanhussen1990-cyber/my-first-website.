Chapter 01

What actually is a force?

A force is a push or a pull on an object, caused by its interaction with something else. Forces are vectors — they have both a size and a direction, and that direction matters just as much as the number.

How big is a newton, really?

  • ~1 N — the weight of a small apple in your hand 🍎
  • ~700 N — an average adult's weight on Earth
  • ~4 000 N — a hard punch from a heavyweight boxer
  • ~35 000 000 N — thrust of the Saturn V at liftoff 🚀
  • 3.5 × 10²² N — the Sun's gravitational pull on Earth ☀️

Weight is a force (newtons). Mass is not (kilograms). On the Moon your mass is unchanged, your weight is ~1/6.

Chapter 04

Newton's three laws, animated

Three sentences from 1687 that still describe almost everything you will ever see move.

FIRST LAW · INERTIA

No force → no change

An object keeps doing what it's doing — at rest or moving in a straight line at constant speed — until a net force acts on it. The top puck has friction; the bottom one is on ice.

SECOND LAW · F = ma

Force → acceleration

The same force gives a light object a big acceleration and a heavy object a small one. Acceleration is proportional to force and inversely proportional to mass.

THIRD LAW · PAIRS

Every push pushes back

Forces always come in equal and opposite pairs acting on different objects. The skater pushes the wall; the wall pushes the skater away.

Chapter 07

The four fundamental forces

Every force above is really one of these four in disguise. Friction, tension, the normal force and springs are all electromagnetism between atoms.

Relative strengths, plotted logarithmically. Gravity is ~10³⁶ times weaker than electromagnetism — it only dominates at planetary scale because mass never cancels, while charge does.

Strong nuclear

Binds quarks into protons and protons into nuclei. Range ~10⁻¹⁵ m. Relative strength 1.

Electromagnetic

Between charges. Infinite range. Responsible for chemistry, light, and every contact force. 10⁻².

Weak nuclear

Drives radioactive beta decay and the fusion that powers the Sun. Range ~10⁻¹⁸ m. 10⁻⁶.

Gravitational

Between masses. Infinite range, always attractive, never cancels. 10⁻³⁸.
