The problem

Two thin discs — mass m, radius a and mass 4m, radius 2a — are fixed on a massless rigid rod of length l = √24·a through their centres. The assembly rolls without slipping on a flat floor, spinning about the rod's own axis at ω. L is the angular momentum of the whole assembly about the point O where the rod's axis meets the floor.

Step 1 — the tilt angle (everything depends on it)

Each disc's plane is perpendicular to the rod, so a disc tilted by θ touches the floor with its centre at height r·cos θ, not r. That single fact is what makes the numbers come out clean.

height difference of centres: l·sin θ = 2a·cos θ − a·cos θ = a·cos θ ⇒ tan θ = a / l = a / (√24·a) = 1/√24 ⇒ sin θ = 1/5 , cos θ = √24/5 = 2√6/5 , θ ≈ 11.54°

Check: the two contact points sit at 5a and 10a from O, and the disc centres at √24·a and 2√24·a along the axis — so both discs and O lie on one straight contact line. The assembly is a rolling cone with apex O.

Step 2 — rolling ⇒ precession (option a)

The contact line is instantaneously at rest, so the total angular velocity must point along that line. Write it as spin about the rod plus precession about z:

ω_total = ω·n̂ + Ω·ẑ must be horizontal z-component: ω·sin θ + Ω = 0 ⇒ |Ω| = ω·sin θ = ω/5 ✓ option (a) is TRUE

Step 3 — centre of mass

d₁ = √24·a , d₂ = 2√24·a (centres, measured from O along the axis) r_cm = (m·d₁ + 4m·d₂)/5m = 9√24·a/5 = 18√6·a/5 ≈ 8.82a distance from the z-axis: ρ = r_cm·cos θ = 216a/25 = 8.64a speed: v_cm = ρ·Ω = 216aω/125 ≈ 1.728aω

Step 4 — the three angular-momentum options

(b) angular momentum of the CM about O

|L_orbital| = M·r_cm·v_cm = 5m · (18√6a/5) · (216aω/125) = 3888√6/125 · ma²ω ≈ 76.2 ma²ω ≠ 81 ma²ω ✗ FALSE

(c) angular momentum about the centre of mass

Resolve ω_total along the rod and perpendicular to it:

ω∥ = ω·cos²θ = 24ω/25 ω⊥ = ω·sin θ·cos θ = 2√6·ω/25 I∥ = ½ma² + ½(4m)(2a)² = 17ma²/2 I⊥ = [¼ma² + m(4√24a/5)²] + [¼(4m)(2a)² + 4m(√24a/5)²] = 469ma²/20 L∥ = I∥·ω∥ = 204ma²ω/25 ≈ 8.16 ma²ω L⊥ = I⊥·ω⊥ = 469√6·ma²ω/250 ≈ 4.60 ma²ω |L_cm| = √(L∥² + L⊥²) ≈ 9.36 ma²ω

Careful. The official key marks (c) TRUE, because I∥·ω = 17ma²ω/2 = 8.5 ma²ω — that is only the component along the rod. The full magnitude, including the perpendicular part, is 9.36 ma²ω. ALLEN's official solution flags exactly this and gives the answer as “(A, D) or (D)”.

(d) the z-component of L

I⊥,O = [¼ma² + m(√24a)²] + [¼(4m)(2a)² + 4m(2√24a)²] = 1649ma²/4 L_z = ω·sin θ·cos²θ · (I∥ − I⊥,O) = (24ω/125)(17/2 − 1649/4)ma² = −1938/25 · ma²ω ⇒ |L_z| ≈ 77.5 ma²ω ≠ 55 ma²ω ✗ FALSE

Equivalently: orbital part Mρ²Ω = 74.65 ma²ω plus spin part 2.87 ma²ω (same sense) = 77.52 ma²ω.
