The nursery

Four acres of roses on the slope below the Wrekin. Grown in the ground, peat-free, and sold by the people who grew them.

Susan Mary has run the nursery since 1994. It is still her, four growers, a glasshouse full of cuttings and a tractor older than all of them. Everything we sell, we grew here.

Bare-root season

Roses lifted from the field while they sleep, wrapped in straw and posted all over the country.

Pruning

Every bush cut back by hand. Free demonstrations in the yard, Saturdays at eleven.

Potting on

Young plants move into peat-free compost and out onto the standing beds.

First flush

The whole field in flower. Rose Weekend is 20–21 June, with tea in the glasshouse.

Cuttings

Next year’s roses are taken from this year’s wood. Propagation classes every Saturday.

Orders open

The bare-root list opens on 1 October. The hedgerow roses are left in hip for the birds.

Rest

The field sleeps. The shop stays open for wreaths, vouchers and gifts until the 23rd.

The grower

“I have never sold a rose I wouldn’t plant in my own garden. If it isn’t ready, it stays in the field another year.”

Susan started with forty cuttings from her grandmother Mary’s garden and a borrowed field behind the village church. Thirty-two years on, she still takes every cutting herself.
