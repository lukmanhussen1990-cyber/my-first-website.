What's moving on screen

Sunlight (energy)

Yellow beams stream from the sun into the leaf. Light is the energy source that powers the whole reaction.

Carbon dioxide (CO₂)

Grey particles drift in from the air through tiny leaf pores called stomata. This supplies the carbon.

Water (H₂O)

Blue droplets rise up the stem from the roots, delivering water and hydrogen to the leaf.

Oxygen (O₂)

Cyan bubbles are released back into the air — the oxygen we breathe is a by-product.

Glucose (food)

Orange sugar collects in the leaf. This stored chemical energy feeds the plant and everything that eats it.

Chlorophyll

The green pigment inside the leaf's chloroplasts absorbs light and makes the reaction possible.

The two stages, step by step

1

Light-dependent reactions (in the thylakoid membranes)

Chlorophyll captures sunlight and uses it to split water (H₂O). This releases oxygen (O₂) as waste and stores energy in two carrier molecules, ATP and NADPH. Think of this stage as charging the plant's batteries.

2

Light-independent reactions — the Calvin cycle (in the stroma)

Using the ATP and NADPH from stage 1, the plant grabs carbon dioxide from the air and assembles it into glucose (C₆H₁₂O₆). No light is needed here directly — just the energy stored earlier.

3

Why it matters

Photosynthesis is the foundation of nearly every food chain and the source of the oxygen in our atmosphere. Plants also lock away carbon, helping regulate Earth's climate. Try the sliders above: with little light, water, or CO₂, the rate meter drops — because the reaction can only run as fast as its scarcest ingredient (the "limiting factor").
