Better Everyday Tea

Same ingredients you already use: tea, water, and (if you like) milk and sugar. The difference is all in the technique.

Ingredients (1 cup)

  • 1 cup (250 ml) fresh cold water
  • 1 tea bag or 1 heaped teaspoon loose black tea
  • Milk and sugar, optional, to taste

Method

  1. Use fresh water. Pour fresh cold water into the kettle. Don't reuse water that's already been boiled, because re-boiled water tastes flat.
  2. Warm the cup or pot. Swirl a little hot water in it, then pour it out. That way the tea doesn't cool down as soon as the water goes in.
  3. Get the water temperature right. Black tea needs water that has just boiled (about 95–100 °C). Green tea is better at 75–80 °C, so let the kettle rest about 3 minutes first.
  4. Put the tea in first, then the water. Pour the water over the tea so the leaves move around and release their flavor.
  5. Cover it and time it. Put a saucer on top so the heat and aroma stay in. Steep black tea for 3–5 minutes and green tea for 2–3 minutes. Steeping too long makes it bitter.
  6. Don't squeeze the bag. Lift it out and let it drip. Squeezing pushes bitter tannins into the cup.
  7. Add milk and sugar last. Stir sugar in while the tea is hot so it dissolves fully. Then add milk a little at a time until the color looks right to you.

For milk tea / chai-style (cooked on the stove)

  1. Bring the water to a boil and add the tea. Simmer it for 2 minutes to get strong color and flavor.
  2. Add the milk and sugar and bring it back to a gentle boil.
  3. Let it rise up in the pan, lower the heat, and repeat 2–3 times. This makes it creamier and richer.
  4. Strain it and pour it from a little height to get a light froth.

Biggest improvements: fresh water, the right temperature, a timed steep, and no squeezing.
