This is Mars, rising beside our Moon. It looks twice as wide.
The rule: a fast flyby at the Moon's distance. No crash. Only physics.
Today, Mars is a tiny dot. At one million kilometers, it looks three-quarters as wide as the Moon.
At Moon distance, it can outshine a full Moon several times over.
Mars has nearly nine times the Moon's mass, so its tides pull nearly nine times harder. Oceans may rise several meters.
Gravity bends both paths. Mars turns just over one degree. Earth's year barely changes.
Certain: the gravity. Uncertain: flood heights, quakes, and the Moon's fate.
Then Mars leaves. Would you stay and watch, or move inland?
