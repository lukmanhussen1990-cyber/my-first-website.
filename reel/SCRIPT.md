## Fact check (sources)
- Kittens meow to tell their mother they're cold or hungry; adult cats don't really meow at each other, but keep meowing at people "probably because meowing gets people to do what they want" — ASPCA, *Meowing and Yowling*.
- Meows "appear to be rare in cat-to-cat interaction and in cat colonies" but are "typical of cat–human interactions" — Prato-Previde et al. 2020, *Animals* (PMC7765146).
- The meow is hardly ever heard in feral colonies; domestic cats meow much more than feral cats — Bradshaw (Cat Sense; 2016 review), Scientific American.
- Body language, scent, stares, tail positions, hisses and growls between cats — ASPCA / Hill's.
- Each cat learns for itself how to use the meow on its human — Bradshaw ("scientists think" / "seems to" wording used).
- Ellis, Swindell & Burman 2015 (10 owners): owners classified meow context above chance only for their **own** cat (and only some owners); unfamiliar cats were at chance level → "owners did best with their own cat's meows… a stranger's cat? Just guessing."
