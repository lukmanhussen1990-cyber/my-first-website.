# Infected creatures: client visuals

These are the resource-pack visuals for the six converted creatures (SPEC §3): `pas:infected_villager`, `pas:infected_cow`, `pas:infected_pig`, `pas:infected_sheep`, `pas:infected_chicken` and `pas:infected_human`.

The design goal: each one is still obviously its species at a glance, but it is visibly parasitised and looks hostile. That means glowing eyes, angry brows, blood at the mouth, veins and sores, raw flesh where the parasite burst out, pale parasite heads growing out of the body, and crimson spider-like limbs.

Target build: Bedrock Preview 1.21.0.26 on Android (RenderDragon, no ray tracing). Every vanilla id, format version, Molang query and material used below was looked up in `bedrock-samples` `v1.21.0.26-preview`. `tools/validate.py` re-checks the references.

## How it is built: two passes per entity

| pass | what | where |
|---|---|---|
| **Base pass** | The vanilla species look, **referenced by id/path only**: vanilla geometry, vanilla textures, and vanilla render controllers wherever our client entity can supply all of their `Texture.`/`Geometry.`/`Material.` keys. Vanilla animations and animation controllers. | `addon/resource_pack/entity/pas_infected_<species>.entity.json` |
| **Infection pass** | **Our original art**: a second render controller (`controller.render.pas_infected_overlay`) draws our overlay geometry with our texture. | `models/entity/pas_infected_<species>.geo.json`, `textures/entity/pas/infected_<species>.png` |

### Overlay geometry

* Every bone that carries an overlay cube has the **same name, parent and pivot as the vanilla bone**. So the vanilla animations (`setup`, `quadruped.walk`, `look_at_target`, baby transforms, `villager.general`, humanoid/zombie arm poses, …) move the overlay exactly like the base model. Because the bone hierarchy is identical, Molang `this` in those animations evaluates to the same value.
* Like vanilla `stray_clothes` / `bogged_clothes`, the overlay render controller sets `rebuild_animation_matrices: true`.
* `gen_infected.py --check-vanilla` re-derives the vanilla rest-pose boxes and pivots from the reference and asserts that they match (current result: 0 mismatches).

The overlay has three kinds of parts:

1. **Skin copies.**
   * Each is an axis-aligned copy of a vanilla cube in its rest pose. Bind-pose and cube rotations of 90° are already baked in, so no `bind_pose_rotation` is needed.
   * Inflate = the vanilla cube's own inflate + **0.2**. Examples: sheep wool 1.75 → 1.95, villager robe 0.5 → 0.7. This 0.2 gap is the same one vanilla uses for the bogged hat layer.
   * Each copy is mapped to our texture, which is transparent except for the infection.
2. **Growths**, all in extra bones (`pas_*`) parented to `body`:
   * `pas_g0`, `pas_g1`: blocky parasite heads on a flesh stalk. They have a pale `#e2b6a0` skin with crimson blotches, white eyes and a dark-red maw with teeth. The palette is shared with the parasite creature and its jar icon.
   * `pas_t0a…c` to `pas_t3a…c`: angular crimson/brown spider-limb tendrils with bone-coloured claw tips. Each one is a chain of three bones; the bend is set by the bone rotations.
   * Quadrupeds also get `pas_jaw`, a parasitic lower jaw (child of `head`) hanging open under the muzzle with a tooth row, plus two fangs.
3. **Glow quads**, in bones named `glow_*`:
   * They are zero-depth quads (1.12.0 per-face `north` UV) placed 0.26 units in front of the vanilla eye texels, and on the parasite heads' eyes.
   * The render controller maps `glow*` bones to `Material.glow` = **`spider`**. That is the emissive-alpha material vanilla spiders use for their eyes: `spider.tga` eye texels have alpha 3, and ours use alpha 3 too.
   * Every other bone uses `entity_alphatest`.
   * Both material names are used by vanilla client entities, which the validator's whitelist requires.

The geometry format is `"1.12.0"`. Bone `rotation`, cube `inflate`, zero-size cubes and per-face `uv`/`uv_size` all appear in vanilla 1.12.0 files: hoglin, allay and fishing_hook.

### Texture painting

The textures are generated by `tools/art/infected/painters.py`. All art is original and procedural. Nothing is copied or derived from a Mojang texture.

* Every texel is mapped back to its 3D position on the model, using georender's box-UV conventions. The infection is then painted in model space, so veins and patches continue across cube edges.
* **Mottling**: brown-red blotches from 3D fBm noise, about 8–12 % coverage. There is less on heads, so faces stay readable.
* **Exposed flesh**: raw-flesh patches with a dark torn rim where each parasite bursts out.
* **Veins**: random walks over the surface of each box. They radiate from the burst sites and wrap round edges.
* **Sores**: red or pus-coloured, with dark rims.
* **Blood**: around the mouth or muzzle, with short drips.
* **Eyes**: the vanilla eye texels are left clear for the glow quads. Around them go an angry brow that slants down toward the nose, a dark lower lid and bloody tears.
* The eye positions come from the vanilla head texels: cow and pig at row 3, sheep at row 2, chicken at row 1, villager at head row 6, Steve at row 4.
* Glow colours:
  * Glowing **white eye with a red inner pupil**: cow, pig, sheep, villager, human.
  * **Red** for the chicken, because white would vanish on white feathers.

## Animations

The files are `animations/pas_infected.animation.json` and `animation_controllers/pas_infected.animation_controllers.json`. Every animation is additive. Our animations run **after** the vanilla ones that reset channels with `- this` (`look_at_target`, `villager.general`, `zombie.attack_bare_hand`), so those resets don't cancel them.

### Attack: `animation.pas_infected.<species>.attack`

* It is driven by `variable.attack_time` (0..1 during a melee swing), the same way vanilla melee mobs drive theirs. The controller `controller.animation.pas_infected.attack` enters the attack state when `variable.attack_time > 0.0` and leaves it when it drops to `<= 0.0`, which is the vanilla warden pattern.
* The animation is keyframed and uses `anim_time_update: "math.clamp(variable.attack_time, 0.0, 1.0)"`, so it is always at rest when no swing is in progress.

What each species does:

* **Quadrupeds (cow, pig, sheep)**: a head lunge and bite. The head rears up −24° with the jaw opening +32°, then snaps down +20° and forward 3 units while the jaw clamps shut. The body lurches 1.2 units forward.
* **Chicken**: the head rears back, then pecks +48° and forward 2.5 units, while the wings flare ±70°.
* **Villager**: a two-handed overhead smash. The crossed `arms` bone rises −100°, then slams +28°, while the head and body lean in.
* **Human**: vanilla zombie arms. `animation.zombie.attack_bare_hand` and `animation.humanoid.attack.rotations` handle the swing; we add a head lunge and a 9° body lean.
* **All species**: the tendrils whip forward and the parasite heads lunge.

### Twitch: `animation.pas_infected.<species>.twitch`

* It loops forever.
* Tendrils sway with sines and jerk with `math.pow(math.sin(…), 9.0)` spikes. The parasite heads look around and twitch, the jaw chatters, and the host head gets small jerks.
* The amplitude rises by 80 % while `variable.has_target` is set (engine variable, used by vanilla hoglin, piglin and vindicator).
* The chicken's wings also shiver.

Every Molang name used appears in this build's vanilla files or in `Molang.html`:

* Animation queries: `query.life_time`, `query.modified_move_speed`, `query.is_baby`, `query.variant`, `query.is_sheared`, `query.is_on_ground`.
* Engine variables: `variable.attack_time`, `variable.has_target`, `variable.wing_flap`.
* Math functions: `math.sin`, `math.pow`, `math.clamp`.

## Per species

### Infected villager (`pas_infected_villager.entity.json`, format 1.8.0 like `villager_v2`)

**Base pass:**

* Geometry: `geometry.villager_v2`.
* Render controllers: **vanilla `controller.render.villager_v2_base` + `controller.render.villager_v2_masked`**, reused unchanged. Our entity supplies the same texture keys:
  * `base`…`base6` = `villager2/villager`;
  * the 7 biome textures;
  * the 15 profession textures (`tool_smith`, `weapon_smith`, `stonemason` …).
* Materials: `villager_v2` and `villager_v2_masked`.
* `scripts.scale` 0.9375 and the vanilla `pre_animation` lines (`variable.num_professions`, `variable.profession_index`).
* So the following are all preserved exactly as vanilla does them:
  * profession from `query.variant`;
  * biome from `query.mark_variant`;
  * skin from `query.skin_id`;
  * the baby material switch (`query.is_baby`).
* The trade-level badge controller is left out, because infected villagers do not trade.
* Animation controllers: vanilla `villager_v2.general` (general + look_at_target), `villager_v2.move`, `villager_v2.baby` (head ×1.5).

**Infection pass:**

* Skin copies: head, nose (bleeding), robe at 0.7, the three crossed-arm cubes, legs.
* Hats and robes stay visible. The overlay head sits under the vanilla hat layer, which has inflate 0.5.
* Growths: a parasite head bursting from the upper-right back and a small one on the left shoulder. Two pairs of spider limbs spread from the back.

### Infected cow (`pas_infected_cow.entity.json`, format 1.10.0 like `cow`)

**Base pass:**

* Geometry: `geometry.cow.v1.8`.
* Our `controller.render.pas_infected_cow` uses `Array.skins[query.variant]` with:
  * `textures/entity/cow/cow` (0);
  * `textures/entity/cow/mooshroom` (1, red);
  * `textures/entity/cow/brown_mooshroom` (2).
* The vanilla mooshroom controller indexes red=0/brown=1, and our variant numbering is shifted by one, so it could not be reused.
* Mooshrooms (variant > 0) also get `controller.render.pas_infected_cow_mushrooms`:
  * It is a conditional render controller, the form vanilla player and pufferfish use.
  * It draws crossed planes (2 on the back, 1 on the head) with the vanilla block textures `textures/blocks/mushroom_red` / `mushroom_brown`, referenced by path.
  * We add them because vanilla mooshroom mushrooms are not part of its geometry or texture.
* Animations: vanilla `cow.setup`, `quadruped.walk`, `common.look_at_target`, `cow.baby_transform`.

**Infection pass:**

* Skin copies: body, udder, head, horns, legs.
* Parasitic jaw and fangs under the muzzle.
* Two parasite heads on the back.
* Four spider limbs from the shoulders and hips.

### Infected pig (format 1.10.0 like `pig`)

**Base pass:**

* **Vanilla `controller.render.pig`**, reused. It needs `Texture.default` and `Texture.saddled`, so `pig_saddle` is supplied; `query.is_saddled` is always 0.
* Geometry: `geometry.pig.v1.8`.
* Animations: `pig.setup`, `quadruped.walk`, `look_at_target`, `pig.baby_transform`.

**Infection pass:**

* Skin copies: body, head, snout, legs.
* Bloody nostrils and maw, jaw and fangs.
* Two parasite heads.
* Four limbs.

### Infected sheep (format 1.10.0 like `sheep`)

**Base pass:**

* **Vanilla `controller.render.sheep`**, reused. It picks `Array.geos[query.is_sheared]` from `geometry.sheep.v1.8` and `geometry.sheep.sheared.v1.8`.
* Material: `sheep`. Texture: `textures/entity/sheep/sheep`.
* This is exactly the vanilla approach: the render controller has no `color` field, and the wool tint comes from the engine's change-colour material, fed from the entity's `minecraft:color`.
* Animations: vanilla `sheep.setup`, `controller.animation.sheep.move` (walk/grazing), `look_at_target`, `sheep.baby_transform`.

**Infection pass:**

* Our `controller.render.pas_infected_sheep_overlay` switches between `geometry.pas.infected_sheep` (wool) and `geometry.pas.infected_sheep.sheared` on `query.is_sheared`.
* Both geometries share one texture.
* Wool version: blood-matted wool copies (inflate 1.95, 0.8 and 0.7 on top of the vanilla wool cubes), plus the face and lower legs. The growths root just under the wool surface.
* Sheared version: skin copies, with the growths lowered to the bare back.

### Infected chicken (format 1.10.0 like `chicken`)

**Base pass:**

* **Vanilla `controller.render.chicken`**, reused, with materials `chicken` and `chicken_legs`.
* Geometry: `geometry.chicken.v1.12`.
* Animations: vanilla `chicken.general`, `chicken.move`, `look_at_target`, `chicken.baby_transform` (head ×2).
* Vanilla chickens get `variable.wing_flap` from the engine. Our entity sets its own in `pre_animation`: the wings flap while the chicken is not on the ground.

**Infection pass:**

* Skin copies: body, head, beak (bloody tip), wattle, wings.
* The legs are skipped: the vanilla leg cubes are mostly transparent, so a solid copy would paint invisible faces.
* One parasite head on the back and two thin limbs.

### Infected human (format 1.8.0 like `zombie`)

**Base pass:**

* `geometry.humanoid.custom`, which is the player model and matches the 64×64 Steve layout, with `textures/entity/steve`.
* Material: `entity_alphatest`, like the player. Steve's hat/jacket/sleeve/pants layers are transparent.
* Render controller: **vanilla `controller.render.zombie`**, reused.
* Animation controllers: vanilla humanoid `look_at_target`, `move`, `attack`, `bob`, plus **`zombie.attack_bare_hand`** (arms forward) and `zombie.swimming`, with the zombie `pre_animation` `variable.tcos0` line.

**Infection pass:**

* Skin copies of head, body, arms and legs.
* A parasite head on the left shoulder and a small one on the upper back.
* Four spider limbs from the back.

**Limitation:** the add-on cannot show the dead player's own skin. Script API 1.11.0 has no skin access, and a client entity cannot reference a player's skin texture. The infected human therefore always wears the default Steve texture under the overlay.

### Babies

* The BP `pas:baby` group sets `minecraft:is_baby` and `minecraft:scale` 0.5.
* The client plays the vanilla baby transform on `query.is_baby`:
  * cow, pig, sheep: head position [0, 4, 4] and scale 2;
  * chicken: head ×2;
  * villager: head ×1.5.
* The overlay head copy, jaw, fangs, glow eyes and the cow's head mushroom are all children of `head`, so they follow the transform. georender confirms this in `babies.png`.
* The growths are children of `body` and scale with the whole entity.

## Regenerating and previews

```bash
python3 tools/art/infected/gen_infected.py                  # geometry, textures, client entities, RCs, animations
python3 tools/art/infected/gen_infected.py --check-vanilla  # copied vanilla boxes/pivots still match the reference
python3 tools/art/infected/preview_infected.py repo         # docs/images/infected_<species>.png
python3 tools/art/infected/preview_infected.py scratch --out <dir outside the repo>
python3 tools/validate.py
```

The generator is deterministic (fixed seeds).

**Repo previews**: `docs/images/infected_{villager,cow,pig,sheep,chicken,human}.png`.

* They show **only our overlay and growth layers**, on a grey stand-in built from our own skin boxes.
* Views: front, iso, back-iso and an attack pose (the sheep also sheared).
* No Mojang texture appears in them.

**Scratch previews** contain vanilla textures, so they are never committed:

| sheet | content |
|---|---|
| `cmp_<species>` | vanilla next to infected |
| `walk_<species>` | walk frames |
| `attack_<species>` | `attack_time` 0 → 0.9 |
| `twitch_<species>` | idle twitch frames |
| `variants_villager` | farmer / librarian / armorer × plains / snow, plus unskilled and baby |
| `variants_cow` | cow, red mooshroom, brown mooshroom, baby |
| `variants_sheep` | 6 colours with the engine tint simulated, sheared, baby |
| `babies` | each species' adult next to its baby |

The walk, attack and twitch sheets confirm that every overlay cube stays attached to its bone while animated.

## Risks and things that cannot be confirmed offline

* **Vanilla id reuse.** We depend on vanilla render controllers (`villager_v2_base`/`_masked`, `pig`, `sheep`, `chicken`, `zombie`), animations, controllers, geometries and texture paths. All exist in 1.21.0.26. If Mojang renames or changes them in a later build (for example new `Texture.` keys in the villager controllers, or a new cow/pig variant system), the base pass will need updating. The overlay itself only depends on bone names and pivots.
* **Sheep wool colour.**
  * This build's Molang documentation has **no `query.color`**, and no vanilla RP file uses one. `docs/ENTITIES.md` assumes the RP can read it.
  * So we follow vanilla exactly: the `sheep` change-colour material with no render-controller `color`, relying on the engine to tint from `minecraft:color`.
  * That this also happens for a custom entity is likely, but **unverified offline**.
  * If in-game wool stays white, the fallback needs a BP change: also set e.g. `minecraft:mark_variant` = colour in `pas:color_<n>`. The RP would then set the render controller `color` from `query.mark_variant`.
* **`variable.attack_time` / `variable.has_target`** are engine variables. Vanilla non-humanoid mobs (warden, hoglin, bee) read them. For custom entities whose melee comes from `behavior.melee_box_attack`, we expect them to be filled in the same way, but this cannot be confirmed offline. If they are not filled in, the bite/swipe simply never plays and everything else is unaffected.
* **Glow.** The `spider` material's behaviour (alpha ≈ 3 renders full-bright, everything else opaque) is inferred from `spider.tga`. The material binary is not in bedrock-samples. georender also does not render bloom or the in-game lighting.
* **Z-fighting.** The overlay sits 0.2 units outside the base, and the glow quads 0.26 units. georender's exact z-buffer cannot show z-fighting, but vanilla uses the same 0.2 offset for the bogged hat layer, so we expect it to hold on Mali-G57.
* **Mooshroom mushrooms** use block textures as entity textures, referenced by path. This is expected to work, but is not demonstrated by any vanilla client entity.
* **georender limitations**: `relative_to` is ignored, materials come from a best-guess table, `minecraft:scale` 0.5 is not applied to babies, and the engine wool tint is simulated by hand in the previews.
